Southern Watch

Foreign and security brief from an Australian vantage point. Each story pairs the reporting with how Beijing, Moscow, Tehran or Pyongyang tell it.

Bottom line up front
  1. Washington has extended its Iran blockade to land routes and the Caspian (Task Force Iron Gate). Trump says a return to full-scale war is "highly unlikely" if Tehran deals. Hormuz is still effectively shut and Brent is near US$98.
  2. Russia blacked out more than 850,000 Ukrainian customers on the eve of the Abu Dhabi talks (10–11 Nov). Moscow says Europe has no seat at the table.
  3. Democrats now control both houses of Congress. Albanese says nothing changes in how he deals with Trump, but AUKUS funding and Iran war powers now face a hostile Congress.

US extends Iran blockade to land routes as Hormuz stays shut

What happened
  • 7 Nov: CENTCOM announced Task Force Iron Gate, a roughly 20-person, Tampa-based cell that extends the naval blockade to Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea. CENTCOM hasn't said whether it will run direct military operations. CENTCOM says the blockade has redirected 144 vessels and kept more than US$25b of Iranian crude off the market.
  • 7 Nov: US forces disabled the St Kitts-flagged cargo ship M/V Huda, which CENTCOM says tried to breach the blockade. Iranian media reported two missiles hit Qeshm Island after what Trump called "minor strikes".
  • 8 Nov: Trump told CNN that resuming all-out attacks was "highly unlikely" if Iran signs a strong deal and reopens Hormuz. He also said the US has "38 bridges" targeted and three carriers in the region. Rubio said the sides are not "on the verge of a breakthrough".
  • Shipping: Iran says two more tankers hit mines on 7 Nov. The UAE rescued 22 crew from a burning cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman. UK maritime authorities logged at least 30 attacks on merchant ships in October, a record.
  • Brent fell 64 cents to US$97.67 in early Asian trade after Trump's remarks. Trump meets Qatar's PM in Washington on Monday (US time).
Why it matters for Australia

Australia imports almost all of its liquid fuel, mainly refined product from South Korea, Singapore and Malaysia, whose refineries depend on Gulf crude. Brent near US$100 feeds directly into petrol, diesel and the RBA's inflation fight. On 7 Nov Albanese said the war was a factor in the US midterm result and that he had told Trump directly "it's time for there to be a de-escalation." Earlier this year Marles said any ADF role in reopening Hormuz depends on a permanent ceasefire. A US land blockade makes that condition harder to meet.

Adversary lens · Tehran and Moscow

Iran's military dismissed Iron Gate as "wordplay". Tehran Times calls it "another unsuccessful attempt to cripple the country's economy". Its Hormuz headline reads "The Strait is under maintenance!", treating the closure as a managed Iranian choice rather than a crisis. FM Araghchi says Iran's two-day proposal stands. His line is: "choose war, and see our... Warriors rain down hell." He says mediation stalled after the US midterms. Moscow is offering Iran a way out of the squeeze: Putin met Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei on 6 Nov to discuss a Russia–Iran gas pipeline. TASS mostly relays Trump and Rubio's own mixed signals. No fresh Chinese statement found. In April Beijing called the port blockade "dangerous and irresponsible".

Angle to build

Iron Gate targets Iran's routes overland and across the Caspian, which run through Russia and Central Asia and link to China. A blockade on those routes is aimed at Moscow and Beijing as much as at Tehran. Ask whether this widens the war into great-power friction Australia can't stay out of, and whether Canberra's push for de-escalation has any leverage in Washington.

Russia hits Ukraine's power grid on the eve of Abu Dhabi talks

What happened
  • Overnight 7–8 Nov: DTEK says more than 600,000 residents in Dnipropetrovsk lost power. Nearly 250,000 customers lost power in Kharkiv region. In Zhytomyr, 140 apartment blocks lost heating. Temperatures are near freezing.
  • Ukraine's air force says it downed 137 of 164 drones launched from the evening of 7 Nov.
  • Zelensky raised the death toll from the 2 Nov strike on an aid centre at Komyshuvakha (Zaporizhzhia) to 55, saying "more people may still be under the rubble".
  • US envoy Witkoff says working-level talks between the US, Russia and Ukraine will run in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 Nov, covering an end to the war, post-war security arrangements and de-escalation. Witkoff and Kushner lead for the US. Zelensky accuses Moscow of escalating before the talks.
Why it matters for Australia

Australia sanctions Russia and has supplied Bushmasters to Ukraine. A deal that rewards taking territory by force, or trades sanctions relief for a ceasefire, sets a precedent for the Indo-Pacific. Australia may also have to decide whether to keep its own sanctions if Washington drops its. The US is also running separate talks with Europeans in Brussels on security guarantees and reconstruction, where Australia has no seat.

Adversary lens · Moscow

The Kremlin's line is that Abu Dhabi is a US–Russia channel and Europe is excluded. Putin aide Ushakov said Europeans can come "if they buy tickets". TASS headlines the foreign ministry saying Europeans "have no place at Ukraine negotiating table". TASS also runs RDIF chief Dmitriev claiming Zelensky has always been against Trump and peace. The strikes on the grid aren't framed as escalation at all in TASS's top coverage.

Angle to build

Moscow is pressuring Ukraine with strikes while presenting itself as the reasonable negotiator. Ask whether a ceasefire made between the US and Russia without Europe would weaken the idea, which Australia also relies on, that allies have a say in their own security.

Democrats take Congress: what it means for the alliance and AUKUS

What happened
  • Democrats won control of both the House and Senate at the 3 Nov midterms. AP tallies cited by Yahoo/CBS had them at about 54 Senate seats and 254 House seats, with races still being counted. Alaska and Mississippi Senate races are too close to call, so totals are provisional.
  • Hakeem Jeffries is in line to become Speaker. Schumer has promised "legislative razor wire" against Trump. Reports say the House could move to impeach Defense Secretary Hegseth (unconfirmed timing).
  • Albanese called the result "very significant", saying the Iran war and tariffs were "major factors". He said: "I will work constructively with President Trump."
Why it matters for Australia

Congress controls US submarine-building money and has to approve transfers under AUKUS. Support for AUKUS has been bipartisan (Rep. Joe Courtney co-chairs the Friends of Australia Caucus). But a Democratic Congress focused on oversight of the Iran war and Hegseth's Pentagon could slow defence bills. A domestic AUKUS inquiry recently called for the program to be abandoned, and the government rejected its findings. Expect the Coalition to push Albanese to engage harder with Trump on Iran.

Adversary lens · Beijing and Moscow

Beijing kept it formal. Spokesperson Guo Jiakun said the midterms "are a domestic affair". He offered to build "a constructive relationship of strategic stability" with the US. This casts China as the steady partner and the US as the volatile one. TASS mainly amplifies Trump's own complaint that the result sent the US "back into the past". No statement found from either government referring to Australia or AUKUS in this context.

Angle to build

AUKUS was sold as insulated from US politics. Test that: identify which AUKUS steps (Virginia-class transfer, shipyard funding, export-control waivers) need a vote or money from Congress in 2027. Then ask what Canberra does if Iran war oversight takes up Congress's attention.

Japan's security rewrite draws Beijing's fire as Wong and Motegi talk Pacific

What happened
  • Tokyo aims to finish revising its three core security documents (National Security Strategy, National Defense Strategy, Defense Buildup Program) by year's end. Reports from October say a 3.5% of GDP spending target will be left out because it would require tax rises, but spending will still grow. Issues under review include the three non-nuclear principles and nuclear-powered submarines.
  • 3 Nov: Penny Wong and FM Motegi met for 15 minutes at the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta. They reaffirmed security and economic-security cooperation with a focus on the Pacific Islands, marking 50 years of the 1976 Basic Treaty.
  • PLA activity around Taiwan has eased after last week's mass deployment: Taiwan's MND counted 4 aircraft, 6 PLAN ships and 2 official ships to 6am on 8 Nov, compared with 32 aircraft on 1 Nov.
Why it matters for Australia

Japan is Australia's closest security partner after the US. A Japan that spends more, has more strike capability and possibly nuclear-powered submarines changes the balance in the region and adds weight to AUKUS. Joint Japan–Australia work in the Pacific gives island states an option between Washington and Beijing.

Adversary lens · Beijing

Spokesperson Guo Jiakun (5 Nov) said the revision reveals a drive toward "remilitarization". He called Japan's framing of China as its "greatest strategic challenge" "nothing but a pretext for Japan to engage in military expansion." He said it "seriously threaten[s] regional peace and stability" and urged other countries to push back. Global Times carried the same line. No mention of Australia in that briefing.

Angle to build

Beijing frames Japan's rearmament as a revival of wartime militarism. It doesn't use that framing for Australia's own build-up (AUKUS, long-range strike). Ask why Beijing treats the two US allies differently, and whether closer defence ties with Japan will draw Australia into the 'militarism' narrative.

PNG: Chinese 'artillery shell' or 'wine bottle'?

What happened
  • 30 Oct: PNG police and the PNG Defence Force recovered a suspected projectile from a container of building materials at a Port Moresby construction site. Taiwan's CNA reports a 32 kg, 130 mm Chinese Type 59 round and four Chinese nationals arrested (arrests not independently confirmed).
  • 31 Oct: Police dismissed suggestions it was a dummy.
  • 3 Nov: The Chinese embassy said it was a "shell-shaped wine bottle" that "poses no danger".
  • The ABC, citing unnamed sources, says it is a real shell with explosive and a fuse. The US military has engaged with PNG bomb-disposal teams. PNG police have not yet released official findings.
Why it matters for Australia

PNG is Australia's closest neighbour and a treaty partner under the 2025 Pukpuk mutual defence treaty. Any sign of Chinese military materiel moving through PNG supply chains touches Australia's core security interest. The US role in the probe also shows Washington taking part in PNG security work alongside Canberra.

Adversary lens · Beijing

The embassy, via Global Times, said it is "virtually impossible" for a military shell to be smuggled out of China, citing China's firearms laws. It said it is awaiting PNG police findings. Global Times named no Western outlets and didn't mention Australia, keeping it a narrow consular matter rather than a geopolitical one.

Angle to build

If police confirm a live round, the embassy's 'wine bottle' line becomes a credibility problem for Beijing in the Pacific. Watch how Port Moresby handles it. The way it balances Australia, the US and China is a good test of how far PNG will hedge.
