PLA mass far-seas deployment around Taiwan, with no exercise name

What happened

Taiwan's NSC chief Joseph Wu reported more than 100 PLA Navy and China Coast Guard vessels in the Western Pacific on 3 Nov. On 2 Nov there were 49 air sorties, the highest single day this year. AEI/ISW analysts count four task groups from the Eastern, Northern and Southern Theatre Commands between Taiwan and Guam. These include the Liaoning group, and the Fujian left Sanya on 2 Nov. Daily counts have dropped back since (4 aircraft, 6 vessels in the 24 hours to 6am on 8 Nov).

Why it matters for Australia

This drill practises keeping outside navies out of the area, which is the job the US alliance and AUKUS force posture would face in a crisis. It also comes ahead of APEC in Shenzhen (18–19 Nov), where Albanese may go on to Beijing (SCMP, unconfirmed). A visit would test how Canberra keeps stabilisation with China going while this is underway.

State media framing · Beijing

The PLA Navy says the Liaoning-led drill is "not directed at any country or target." No name, no announcement. The Taiwan Affairs Office dismisses Taipei's interference claims as DPP "political maneuvering." The message is that this is now routine.

Angle to build

The last four big Taiwan drills were named and announced, and this one wasn't. That makes it hard to tell a drill from preparations for the real thing. Ask what this does to Australian and US warning time, and whether Defence's planning around warning time still holds.

Hormuz still closed as mines hit tankers and Qatar mediates

What happened

Iran says two tankers caught fire after hitting mines on an "unauthorised route." Explosions were heard on Qeshm Island and at Jask, days after US strikes. CENTCOM disabled a St Kitts-flagged ship in the Gulf of Oman. Trump called Friday's strikes on Iranian air defences "minor" and is weighing a larger wave now that the midterms are over. The IRGC lifted its self-imposed 2,000 km ballistic missile range cap. Iran is reviewing Qatari proposals on its nuclear program.

Why it matters for Australia

Australia imports most of its liquid fuel, so a long closure hits the economy directly. The RBA's 25-point hike to 4.85% cited war-driven energy costs. The ADF has given defensive support to Gulf states since March, so any escalation after the midterms raises the question of how far that commitment goes.

State media framing · Tehran and Beijing

Tehran Times quotes FM Araghchi saying Iran is ready for "both defense and diplomacy" and that the choice rests with Washington. Iran's plan has the US move first (lift the naval blockade, release frozen assets) and the strait reopen afterwards. Beijing has signed a joint statement with Oman backing reopening, which lets it look like the stabiliser.

Angle to build

Closing the strait is Iran's main source of leverage, so mines are worth more to it than a deal. Track whether China gets diplomatic credit (and oil) out of Gulf instability while Australia carries the costs as a US ally.

Abu Dhabi talks set for 10–11 Nov after Trump meets Putin's envoy

What happened

Trump met Kirill Dmitriev at the White House and said a deal may be close, but gave no details and didn't meet the Ukrainian delegation. Witkoff says working-level talks between Russia, the US and Ukraine will cover post-war security arrangements, de-escalation and humanitarian issues. A Kremlin aide says sanctions relief for Russia's sovereign wealth fund is being discussed. The EU's Kallas rejects any deal made without Europe.

Why it matters for Australia

Australia has sanctions on Russia and has sent Bushmasters to Ukraine. A deal that rewards taking territory by force and lifts sanctions sets a precedent that would be read closely in the Indo-Pacific. Australia would also have to decide whether to keep its own sanctions if Washington drops its.

State media framing · Moscow

TASS covers this as Russia and the US talking directly as equals. Medinsky leads the delegation, Putin briefed the Security Council on 6 Nov, and Moscow says it may reconsider after Ukrainian attacks. Defence Minister Belousov also calls Russia–China ties "unprecedentedly high."

Angle to build

Look at who negotiates for Russia. Dmitriev (sovereign wealth fund) is running the economic side and Medinsky the political side. Moscow is trading sanctions relief for a ceasefire. Ask which Australian sanctions would come under pressure first.

North Korea's fourth weapons launch in a month

What happened

KCNA reported cluster-bomb warhead tests (1 Nov), a strategic cruise missile with a dispersal warhead (2 Nov), and a new air-defence missile plus a cruise missile strike drill (4 Nov). NK News says the drill photos were heavily doctored. Talks with Washington remain in doubt.

Why it matters for Australia

The ADF regularly sends aircraft and ships to monitor UN sanctions on North Korea. Australia is also strengthening ties with Japan and South Korea. Cluster and dispersal warheads point to battlefield use against airfields, which also matters for how Australia thinks about protecting its own bases.

State media framing · Pyongyang and Beijing

KCNA calls the launches routine training of units. Wang Yi calls Xi's June visit to Pyongyang the start of a "new era." AEI reads Beijing as making room for the North's nuclear program instead of restraining it.

Angle to build

Russia, China and North Korea are increasingly backing each other. Ask whether Australian planning still treats the Korean Peninsula and Taiwan as separate problems.

The Pacific contest: Solomons treaty, PNG explosive, Quad in Atlanta

What happened
  • Canberra and Honiara have an in-principle treaty, reportedly with A$980m in extra funding. Solomon Islands keeps its security pact with China, and PM Wale faces a no-confidence motion.
  • The ABC says a Chinese military-grade explosive found in a container in PNG is a real weapon. Chinese officials had called it a wine bottle.
  • Quad foreign ministers (31 Oct, Atlanta) raised concerns about economic coercion and export restrictions, and announced critical minerals work and port cooperation in Fiji. A leaders' summit is planned for Manila this month.
  • The ADF sent a C-130, a Citation and 20 personnel to help fight the East Kalimantan fires.
Why it matters for Australia

Pacific security partnerships are where Australia competes most directly with China for influence, and where Canberra has the most to lose if partners hedge.

State media framing · Beijing

Global Times consistently casts AUKUS and Pacific security deals as Australia being used as a "US chess piece." It amplifies Australia Institute polling (59% favour a more independent foreign policy) and the community AUKUS inquiry, which Canberra rejected on 4 Nov.

Angle to build

Beijing's message to Australians is to drift away from the US, not to fear China. Democrats now control both chambers of Congress, so AUKUS funding will get more US scrutiny. Expect Chinese state media to use that as evidence the US is unreliable.
