Kremlin says Putin–Xi–Trump summit will happen in Shenzhen on 19 November

What happened
  • 14 Nov: Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told TASS a trilateral summit of Putin, Xi and Trump "will take place in Shenzhen on November 19", on the sidelines of the APEC leaders' meeting (18–19 Nov).
  • SCMP reported on 14 Nov that the White House had confirmed the meeting. TASS also cites a White House spokesperson saying Trump intends to meet both leaders.
  • 13 Nov: China's foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said only that China "is in communication with relevant parties" about a meeting of the three leaders. Beijing has not confirmed a date.
  • RT, citing AP, says the talks are expected to focus on "nuclear arms control and international security", including Ukraine and Iran. The digests report TASS quoting Rubio that the US wants nuclear weapons on the agenda. I did not see that quote in the original.
  • On 13 Nov, the first US–China Trade Council meeting was held by video. Xinhua says it covered tariffs, a reciprocal "30 billion for 30 billion" tariff-cut deal and farm trade. Separately, Wang Yi called for "uninterrupted" dialogue with Washington.
  • Albanese attends APEC in Shenzhen and then holds the Australia–China Annual Leaders' Meeting in Beijing.
Why it matters for Australia

A leaders' meeting of the three nuclear powers, with arms control on the agenda, would be the first of its kind. It would happen in China, with Xi as host. Australia would not be in the room, but its interests are directly at stake: limits or no limits on China's fast-growing arsenal, Iran, and a Ukraine settlement. The meeting also lifts Beijing's standing just before Albanese's own talks in Beijing. If Trump trades on trade, chips or Taiwan language in Shenzhen, Canberra will have to recalibrate quickly. Watch whether Washington briefs its allies before the meeting or after it.

State media framing · Moscow and Beijing

Moscow is selling the meeting hard as diplomacy among equals. RT quotes Ushakov's hope that a "first meeting in [this] format will have a positive impact". TASS stresses the summit date and the US wish to discuss nuclear weapons. The Russian MFA highlighted US officials signing a condolence book for Ambassador Darchiev, a signal of warming ties. Beijing is more cautious. Its foreign ministry is only "in communication". People's Daily and CGTN keep the focus on APEC as a showcase for an Asia-Pacific "community" and Shenzhen innovation. They frame the dialogue with the US as sending "positive signals". Chinese state media did not lead with the trilateral in the coverage I found. That fits a host who wants APEC, not Putin, to be the story.

Angle to build

China has long refused trilateral nuclear arms control. Why would Beijing now host a meeting where Moscow and Washington say arms control is on the table? Is Xi buying status at low cost, or is something being traded? Track what the Chinese readout says about "strategic stability".

Iran claims more mined tankers and a 17th US drone downed in Hormuz; no independent confirmation

What happened
  • 14 Nov: The IRGC Navy said two "offending" tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines on an "unauthorized" route through the southern Strait of Hormuz. It said they had switched off radar and positioning systems. Separately, Iran's regular Navy said a tanker hit a mine in a "prohibited area" east of the strait. It is unclear whether these are three ships or overlapping claims.
  • The Navy statement, carried by Tasnim, said "fire and explosions are the fate of tankers involved in illegal activities" (Middle East Monitor). No ship name, flag or casualty figure has been given.
  • 14 Nov: The IRGC said it shot down a 17th US MQ-1 drone over Qeshm Island. The Army Navy separately claimed an MQ-1 near Kuhestak, Hormozgan. Both claims are unconfirmed.
  • CENTCOM has not confirmed any of the 13–14 Nov claims. It has previously called similar IRGC mine claims false and says the main transit lanes are clear.
  • Israel's Kan reported that CENTCOM told the IDF to prepare to resume operations against Iran. TASS notes CENTCOM told Al Jazeera it sent no such instruction.
  • 13 Nov, UN Security Council: Iran's chargé d'affaires Gholamhossein Darzi said "the United States is the aggressor, not the victim". He said Washington must "immediately and unconditionally" end its aggression and blockade.
  • Iraq says it is resuming oil exports through Hormuz under US protection (TASS). Iran International reports Iran's October oil output fell by nearly half from pre-war levels.
Why it matters for Australia

Australia has an E-7A Wedgetail committed to the UK- and France-led defensive Hormuz mission. Tehran said this week that any state backing the US at sea will be treated as a party to the war. Mine claims, true or not, keep war-risk insurance and freight high on the route that feeds the Asian refineries Australia relies on for diesel and jet fuel. The claims also seem designed to create a de facto Iranian licensing regime for the strait. That cuts against freedom-of-navigation principles Australia defends in the South China Sea.

State media framing · Tehran

Iranian outlets present the mining as lawful policing of sovereign waters. Tankers are "violating" or "offending" ships in a "prohibited area", and others are told they "will face the same fate". Vice President Ansari says Iran wants respect for its "authority over the Strait of Hormuz". Mehr calls the war a "turning point" for world order and US hegemony. Press TV pushes the line that the US is "agreement-incapable". TASS relays the drone claim without comment, and Lavrov discussed Gulf tensions in Abu Dhabi.

Angle to build

Iran's claims run ahead of the evidence: no names, no flags and no satellite images. Yet the threat still works on insurers. How much of Iran's Hormuz strategy is information operations rather than mines in the water? How should a navy, or the RAN, plan around uncertainty that is itself the weapon?

Zelenskyy says US floated 'Donbas for NATO' as Europe names its team and support frays

What happened
  • 14 Nov (ABC/Reuters): Zelenskyy said US negotiators asked: "What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?" He said this is the first time NATO membership has been linked to territory. He called the talks "extremely difficult" and repeated he would not concede land, though a full package could go to a referendum.
  • He also said Ukraine will not write neutrality into its constitution (TASS).
  • EU leaders agreed a European negotiating team: EU institutions plus the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Poland (Costa, via Xinhua). Europeans met US and Ukrainian officials in Abu Dhabi but did not sit with Russia.
  • Moscow wants a broad European security agreement settled before the terms for ending the war. Merz says NATO already fills that role. Zakharova replied that NATO "undermines the immunity of Europe".
  • Poland's Tusk said Warsaw will not fund more PURL purchases of US weapons for Ukraine "at this time". Bulgaria's PM Radev said Sofia will send no more arms from its stocks.
  • Russia's MoD claims it captured Tersyanka (Zaporizhzhia) and struck a cargo ship carrying military equipment at Chornomorsk. These claims are unverified. ABC reports on merchant ships being sunk in the Black Sea.
Why it matters for Australia

Australia contributes to PURL, the NATO mechanism that buys US weapons for Ukraine (RT lists Canberra among non-NATO contributors). If larger European donors pull back, there will be more pressure on outside partners to fill gaps. A 'land for alliance membership' formula would be a striking precedent. It treats territory and alliance status as tradeable, a logic Beijing could later apply to Taiwan or the first island chain. Black Sea shipping strikes also set norms on attacks on merchant vessels, which matter to a maritime trading nation.

State media framing · Moscow (and Beijing)

Moscow presents Europe as the obstacle and Western support as crumbling. RT says Poland and Bulgaria "put brakes" on Ukraine weapons. It cites a Pentagon inspector-general report to argue Ukraine aid is hurting US readiness, and repeats claims that Kyiv glorifies Nazi collaborators. TASS leads on Zelenskyy refusing neutrality, not on the NATO-for-Donbas idea. Envoy Polyansky says Europeans "did not mention the Abu Dhabi talks even once". Xinhua gives Europe's line more space, quoting Merz ("no discussion about Ukraine without Ukraine"), but stresses that "mutual trust is in doubt" between Europe and the US.

Angle to build

Each outlet chose a different headline from the same Zelenskyy remarks: ABC led on Donbas for NATO, TASS on no neutrality, Xinhua on rifts with Europe. This is a good exercise in selective framing. Which version would you brief to a minister, and why?

Beijing steps up pressure on Japan as Washington asks Tokyo to triple troop-hosting payments

What happened
  • 13 Nov: China's defence ministry spokesperson Chen Xi said Japan's planned defence spending rise and reported exceptions to its three non-nuclear principles "betray its commitment to peace". Those exceptions would let nuclear weapons be brought into Japan in an emergency. Japan's revised security documents are expected to keep calling China its "greatest strategic challenge" (CGTN).
  • 12 Nov: A foreign ministry spokesperson said accepting the Tokyo Trials verdicts is a "prerequisite" for Japan's postwar standing (digest). CGTN is promoting a new "comfort women" documentary.
  • 13–14 Nov: Nikkei, Jiji and the Japan Times report the US wants Japan to pay more than ¥3 trillion over five years from FY2027 to host US forces. That is roughly triple the current ¥1.06 trillion deal. Tokyo is reportedly weighing about ¥1.5 trillion and finds the demand difficult to accept. Both sides aim for a deal by year's end.
  • Meanwhile, Wang Yi met South Korea's foreign minister Cho Hyun on 13 Nov and pledged deeper ties before APEC.
Why it matters for Australia

Japan is Australia's closest security partner in Asia after the US, with a Reciprocal Access Agreement and the Mogami frigate deal. Beijing's campaign tries to recast Japanese rearmament as revived militarism and to separate Tokyo from Seoul. The US demand shows how Washington now prices its alliances. It is a preview of the conversation Canberra could face over AUKUS costs, US force posture in northern Australia, and defence spending targets. A Japan debate on hosting nuclear weapons would also test Australia's own non-proliferation stance.

State media framing · Beijing

Beijing links Japan's present-day policy to wartime history: the Tokyo Trials, "comfort women" and "neo-militarism". It also draws Australia in. Global Times covered the 88th anniversary of Port Kembla's 1938 Dalfram Dispute, when wharfies refused to load pig iron for Japan, as Australian-Chinese solidarity against Japanese aggression (via the digest; I did not read the full text). Xinhua relayed the Japanese reports of the US cost demand, casting the alliance as a burden imposed by Washington. In contrast, coverage of Seoul is warm: Xinhua called Gyeongju-to-Shenzhen a "shared vision".

Angle to build

Beijing uses Australian labour history to support an anti-Japan narrative. That makes Australia part of Beijing's soft war-memory campaign. How should Canberra handle a shared anti-fascist history when it is used against a current partner?

US nationwide flight ground stop becomes a 'Western decline' story for Moscow, Tehran and Beijing

What happened
  • 14 Nov (US time): The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop on US departures for about five hours after a telecommunications outage at air traffic control centres. FlightAware counted about 9,800 delays and 4,700+ cancellations.
  • Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed old equipment and overloaded routers. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of intrusion and the problem "appears" mechanical, but investigators are still looking. Trump rejected suggestions that a new FAA AI rerouting system was involved.
  • US-bound departures from Sydney were briefly held. Australians were stranded at US airports (ABC).
  • On the same day, US forces responded to suspected drone activity near RAF bases in England used by the US, and Eurocontrol suspended US-bound departures.
Why it matters for Australia

This was an infrastructure failure, not an attack, as far as officials can tell. But it shows how one fault in an ally's critical systems spreads to Australian travellers and trade within hours. The same fragility applies to the US logistics and command networks the ADF depends on. It also shows the information side: adversary outlets moved within hours to turn a technical failure into a story about the US.

State media framing · Moscow, Tehran and Beijing

The three diverge. Press TV and TASS stressed "decades-old" and "incredibly old" US systems. TASS also paired the story with the US losing its measles-elimination status. Tehran Times went furthest, saying officials' "shifting explanations" deepen doubts over US airspace. Xinhua reported the no-cyberattack statement plainly, alongside other US domestic troubles: Hassett's expected exit, Democratic infighting, and protesters heckling Hakeem Jeffries. Beijing and Moscow are cautious before Shenzhen. Tehran, at war, is not.

Angle to build

The same 'decline' theme comes in different strengths. Tehran suggests a cover-up, Moscow mocks, and Beijing just reports. That pattern tracks each capital's current need for Washington. Use it as a tool: when Beijing's tone toward the US sharpens, what has changed in the relationship?
