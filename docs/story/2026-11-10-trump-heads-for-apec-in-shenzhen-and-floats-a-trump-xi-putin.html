Trump heads for APEC in Shenzhen and floats a Trump–Xi–Putin meeting

What happened
  • Trump says he expects to meet Putin and Xi together during his trip to the APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen on 18–19 Nov: "one of the meetings I think will be with President Putin and President Xi". He says the agenda includes "denuclearization" and ending the Iran war. Peskov says "work is underway". Beijing has announced no trilateral.
  • A Manila stop on 16 Nov has been reported (New York Post, citing the Philippine palace). This is not confirmed by the White House.
  • SCMP reports US military transport aircraft landing in Shenzhen, widely read as preparation for the visit.
  • 10 Nov: China's Commerce Ministry extended for two more months its suspension of export-control measures against 16 US entities (listed April 2025). It said this implements the consensus from the "eighth round" of US–China trade talks. The broader trade truce runs to January.
  • This would be the third Trump–Xi meeting this year, after Beijing in May and Washington in September.
Why it matters for Australia

A great-power deal over Iran or Ukraine struck in China, without allies present, is the 'Yalta 2.0' scenario Lowy's Interpreter warns about. Canberra would want assurance that Taiwan, the South China Sea and the alliance are not part of any trade-off. A stable US–China trade truce helps Australian exporters. A summit that reads as Washington accommodating Beijing would sharpen the domestic AUKUS debate. ASPI this week stressed that the PLA Southern Theater Command, not global Chinese dominance, is Australia's most direct military threat.

State media framing · Beijing and Moscow

Beijing is staging APEC as proof of its openness to trade. CGTN uses the import expo to promote APEC and Global South links, and Xinhua reports more than US$650m in US farm deals signed there. On the US, Xinhua's Chinese feed highlights division: Jeffries says Hegseth "must go", and Democrats plan to roll back "unreasonable" tariffs. On the Nvidia affair, FM spokesman Guo Jiakun called "Chinese spy" claims "pure speculation and slander" (translated). Beijing has not confirmed the trilateral. Moscow, through Peskov, calls it "extremely useful". Lowy argues it has little upside for Beijing with Europe.

Angle to build

Beijing hosting both Trump and Putin lets it play convenor on Iran and Ukraine. What would it want in return, and on which Indo-Pacific issues? How would Canberra detect and respond to a quiet trade-off?
