Saudi–Houthi war escalates: village strike near Sanaa, missiles at Riyadh

What happened
  • 7 Nov: Airstrikes hit al-Shariya village in Bani Hushaysh, a Houthi stronghold east of Sanaa. Houthi-run Saba says at least 33 people were killed and 39 wounded, mostly women and children. Reuters could not verify the figures. Saudi Arabia's Center for International Communication says it is investigating the reports.
  • 8 Nov: The Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at the Eastern Province and two missiles fired at Riyadh. Falling debris damaged an administrative building in Riyadh's Aramco Tower. The Energy Ministry reported limited damage and no casualties. The Houthis also claim attacks on Saudi airports and Aramco storage sites.
  • The fighting resumed in July and ended the 2022 UN-brokered truce. The IOM says more than 200,000 people have been displaced since then.
Why it matters for Australia

With Hormuz effectively shut, Saudi exports that bypass the strait (the East-West pipeline to Red Sea ports) matter more to the Asian refineries that supply Australia's fuel. Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites and a Houthi naval blockade declared against Saudi Arabia threaten that bypass. A wider Gulf war also makes the region harder to stabilise even if a US–Iran deal is reached. That affects any future ADF maritime contribution.

State media framing · Tehran and Moscow

Tehran treats the Houthi strikes as justified retaliation. Tehran Times headlines that Yemen hit the Aramco tower "in response to Saudi attacks". Iran's foreign ministry condemned the Saudi airstrikes and called for them to end, without mentioning Houthi missile fire. Moscow's RT gave prominence to the damage at Aramco Tower and to Saudi civilian casualties. The milblogger channel Rybar claims "Ukrainian UAVs" were shot down over Yemen (unverified). That fits a wider Russian line that ties Kyiv to every war.

Angle to build

Is Iran using the Houthis to pressure Riyadh while it bargains with Washington through Qatar? Is the Saudi–Houthi war now a separate conflict that a US–Iran deal would not end? Your answer shapes how much a Hormuz deal would really ease Australia's fuel risk.
