Iran claims four more tankers hit in a day; top clerical body calls NPT exit an "unavoidable necessity"

What happened
  • Night of 11–12 Nov: The IRGC Navy said two "violating" supertankers carrying more than 4 million barrels were "stopped by massive explosions" after taking an "unauthorized southern route" through the Strait of Hormuz. It said both were burning. It gave no names, flags or casualties, and did not say what caused the blasts (Mehr).
  • 12 Nov, afternoon: Iran's Army said two more tankers hit a naval mine and caught fire in a "prohibited zone" east of the strait, after switching off their radar (Mehr, IRNA).
  • All four claims are unconfirmed. I found no independent or US account for these incidents. CENTCOM has called earlier IRGC mine claims false. I could not find a reliable oil price for 12 Nov.
  • 12 Nov: The Assembly of Experts, the clerical body that chooses the supreme leader, urged officials to examine leaving the Non-Proliferation Treaty. It called exit "an unavoidable necessity and a public and national demand" and asked for a review of defence doctrine to build deterrence. It warned of an immediate response "without geographic limits" to any new attack.
  • Iran International notes the Assembly has no direct say over nuclear policy, but is the first governing body to call for a doctrine review. It says this could open the way to reversing the late Ali Khamenei's religious ban on nuclear weapons. The Foreign Ministry said earlier this week that Iran remains committed to the NPT.
  • Armed forces spokesman Shekarchi said any US attack would get a response "ten times" stronger, after Trump reportedly threatened strikes five times heavier than before (Mehr; I could not confirm Trump's wording). Spokesman Baghaei warned third countries that no agreement with the US gives "legitimate grounds" to join an attack.
  • Cost in the Pacific: briefings to NZ Foreign Minister Peters, released under official information law, put extra fuel costs for Pacific countries at NZ$935 million from late February to the end of July. Tonga's fuel prices are at a record. PNG diesel is at its highest since April after a subsidy ended. The ADB used an emergency grant fund for the first time, for Tonga, Tuvalu and Kiribati.
Why it matters for Australia

Fuel first. Four claimed hits in a day, true or not, raise insurance and freight costs on the route that feeds the Asian refineries Australia buys from. The RNZ figures show where the bill lands: on Pacific budgets that Australia and New Zealand are already propping up, with Fiji acting as the fuel hub for its neighbours. Second, the NPT. Australia has built its non-proliferation policy, and its defence of AUKUS naval reactors, on the treaty. An Iranian exit would be the first since North Korea's in 2003 and would weaken the rules Canberra relies on. Third, Baghaei's warning to "third countries" extends the logic Rezaei used on bases this week: helping a US strike makes you a party.

State media framing · Tehran, with Beijing relaying

Tehran frames the tanker hits as law enforcement, not attacks. The ships are "violating" or "offending", the route "illegal and unsafe", and the IRGC warns any ship that works with the "U.S. smuggling army" will "meet the same fate". The Army blames "the liar U.S. president" first, then owners who "trust his false claims". IRNA ran a legal defence of "defensive maritime measures". On the NPT, Press TV pinned the Assembly's statement and tied it to the failure of global nuclear safeguards. The line is that deterrence is a "public and national demand", not a regime choice. Not everyone at home agrees: Tehran Times relayed Kayhan saying that continuing the present stand-off "could bring adversaries closer to their goals". Global Times relayed the IRGC claim with "violating" in quotation marks, and Xinhua gave straight coverage to an IRGC missile and drone exhibition. None mentioned Australia.

Angle to build

The Assembly cannot order an NPT exit, so why speak now? Test two readings: a bargaining signal to Washington before APEC, or the regime preparing its own public for a doctrine change. What evidence would separate them?
