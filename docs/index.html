Putin lands in Shenzhen as Xi prepares to host Trump and Putin together; Australia watches from the sidelines

What happened
  • APEC leaders meet in Shenzhen on 18–19 November. Putin arrived this morning. Kremlin spokesman Peskov says "everything is agreed" for a Xi–Putin–Trump meeting, which RT and Global Times date to Thursday 19 November. It would be the first time the three leaders have met together.
  • Earlier reporting points to nuclear arms control and the US war on Iran as the main topics. Whether Ukraine is on the agenda is still unclear.
  • Lavrov, speaking in Manila on 17 Nov, said it would be "easy" to hold another such meeting if the leaders see value in it. He would not say whether it should become a regular event.
  • Xi has already held bilateral meetings in Shenzhen with Canada's Carney (fresh tariff relief agreed), Vietnam's To Lam, PNG's Marape, and the leaders of Peru and Chile.
  • In the same news cycle, China's foreign ministry called new US limits on remote access to advanced chips "typical economic coercion", and Global Times says Beijing has vowed "firm countermeasures".
  • Trump has said he "may" meet Kim Jong Un while in China, and the Kremlin says Putin would meet Kim if he attends. No Kim visit has been announced. South Korean officials last week judged a meeting unlikely.
  • The ABC reports Albanese will go to Shenzhen for a CEO roundtable and is scheduled to meet Xi on Friday.
Why it matters for Australia

Australia helped create APEC. Now the forum's headline event is a three-power meeting that Canberra is not part of. Any understanding on nuclear arms, Iran or Ukraine reached there would shape Australia's security environment without Australia having any input. The Albanese–Xi meeting on Friday comes straight after the Quad. That makes it a test of whether Canberra can keep 'stabilisation' with Beijing going while signing up to Quad minerals and defence deals aimed at reducing dependence on China. New US chip controls, and Beijing's promised countermeasures, could also affect Australian trade and technology partnerships.

State media framing · Beijing and Moscow

Beijing and Moscow are running a shared script. RT published a Shenzhen analysis "as part of RT's partnership with Global Times". It rejects Western comparisons to "a second Yalta" and says the summit "is not about dividing zones of influence". It also argues "most major-power risks stem from broken dialogue". Global Times editorials say the Asia-Pacific "roadmap becomes clearer" and that "dialogue still beats barriers". A Xinhua analysis presents China as the anchor of a changing region. TASS is giving the visit wall-to-wall coverage, down to security at Putin's hotel, and uses a US arms-control voice to present the meeting as a chance to halt the nuclear arms race. Chinese state media are much harsher on US chip curbs ("economic coercion") than on Trump himself, whose visit is being handled warmly.

Angle to build

Beijing is staging itself as the convenor of the great powers. Does a Xi–Putin–Trump format weaken the US alliance network, by signalling that the big three settle the big issues, even if nothing concrete is agreed? And how should a middle power like Australia position itself when the region's leading forum is turned into a stage for great-power theatre?

Quad revived in Manila with exercise and minerals deals; Lavrov singles out Australia

What happened
  • Trump, Albanese, Modi and Takaichi met in Manila on the evening of Tuesday 17 November, on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit. It was the first Quad leaders' meeting in two years and the first of Trump's second term.
  • Trump announced a Quad joint military exercise with maritime patrol aircraft, building on recent naval drills near Japan. He also announced new critical minerals deals so that no member is "vulnerable to coercion by any single foreign supplier". China was not named.
  • Leaders pledged more than US$85 million (about A$126 million) to expand AI access among Indo-Pacific partners. Albanese backed a first Quad Business Leaders Forum in 2027.
  • On AUKUS, Trump said the US makes "the greatest submarines" and is "working with Australia".
  • Albanese raised the Iran war's economic impact with Trump, saying Australia is "not protagonists" but wants de-escalation. Asked about China's reaction, he said: "We want peace and security in the region."
  • At the East Asia Summit in Manila the same day, Lavrov attacked "exclusive clubs", "trios" and "quads". At his press conference he said it is "mainly European countries, Australia and Japan that are politicising this forum". TASS also reports he warned that AUKUS activity creates risks for nuclear-weapon-free zones.
  • ASPI argues the Quad depends too much on summits and needs institutional foundations.
Why it matters for Australia

The revival is a win for Canberra: Trump has re-endorsed a grouping Australia has invested in, and mentioned AUKUS without prompting. But no tariff relief was reported, and the new exercise is only a modest step. Being named by Lavrov puts Australia, alongside Japan, in Moscow's 'Asian NATO' storyline. Moscow uses that line to court ASEAN, and Beijing reinforces it. It is also part of a pattern: Russia has repeatedly blamed Canberra for East Asia Summit deadlock, as it did in 2024.

State media framing · Moscow and Beijing

Moscow: Lavrov frames the Quad as an outdated bloc model imposed on ASEAN. He calls on members to defend "ASEAN's centrality" and accuses Western states of hollowing out the consensus-based architecture. He also pitched Russia–ASEAN strategic partnership. RT, ahead of Trump's visit, highlighted Manila protests that called a US minerals deal "a return to colonialism". Beijing: in the digests, Chinese state media gave the summit almost no direct coverage, which fits a wish not to spoil the Shenzhen mood. Before the summit, a Global Times commentary (3 Nov) called the Quad a "loose club" whose projects mostly stayed on paper. It said Manila let Washington signal that "the US is still present", and warned China would oppose any use of it to interfere in the South China Sea. Tehran: Press TV and Tehran Times amplified the arrest of nearly 300 anti-Trump protesters in Manila.

Angle to build

Beijing has stayed quiet while Moscow has been loud. Is there a deliberate split between Beijing, which wants a calm run-up to Shenzhen and Friday's meeting with Albanese, and Moscow, which can afford to attack the Quad in ASEAN forums? Does the 'anti-colonial' framing of US minerals deals work with Southeast Asian audiences?

Gulf flare-up: Iranian drones at Bahrain, US strike on Qeshm, more mine claims at Hormuz

What happened
  • On 17 Nov the IRGC said it targeted a US radar at Bahrain's Isa Air Base. CENTCOM said multiple Iranian one-way attack drones were intercepted, with no US casualties or damage.
  • Hours later, US jets struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island. A US official called it self-defence "in response to IRGC aggression". Iranian officials say there was no damage to residential, commercial or vital infrastructure.
  • Iran claims its forces shot down a US MQ-1 over Qeshm ("the 18th") and a LUCAS drone over Hormuz. The US has not confirmed either.
  • Iran's navy and army report several tankers, including a supertanker and an LPG carrier, hitting mines east of Hormuz, up to four in one day. Iranian outlets give conflicting counts. UKMTO separately reported a vessel struck near Khasab, Oman, with a fire and crew unaccounted for. It is unclear whether these are the same incidents. Independent confirmation of the mine strikes is limited.
  • Rubio condemned the attacks on Bahrain. Saudi Arabia and Qatar called for a halt. A third US carrier group is now in the region.
  • The IRGC warned that Bahrain's rulers have made three "mistakes" that put their survival in doubt. The warning came after Bahrain sentenced Sheikh Isa Qassim and dozens of Shia clerics to life in prison.
  • FM Araghchi was in Muscat for talks with Oman and a Qatari delegation. Qatar says it will discuss proposals to narrow US–Iran differences.
Why it matters for Australia

Any further disruption at Hormuz feeds into Australian fuel prices and the security of the Asian refinery supply that Australia depends on. Albanese told Trump in Manila that Australia is not a party to the war but wants de-escalation. That is the line to watch if Washington asks partners for maritime contributions. ADF personnel and assets in the Gulf region, and Australian seafarers, are exposed to the mine and drone risk.

State media framing · Tehran (with Moscow and Beijing)

Tehran presents the mining as lawful enforcement. Damaged ships are "offending" or "violating" tankers that strayed into a "prohibited zone", and Hormuz will reopen only on Iran's terms. The Qeshm strike is cast as "aggression" that achieved nothing. Speaker Ghalibaf and the foreign ministry pre-emptively dismiss US reports of an Iranian "dirty bomb" plot and of missile bases near dams as "false flag" pretexts. Tehran Times headlined "False flags for a failed war". Moscow lined up behind Tehran: Zakharova expressed "regret" over the escalation, and a Russian envoy called strikes on Iranian nuclear sites illegal. Xinhua relays Iranian denials and US force build-ups without comment.

Angle to build

Iran's 'restricted zone' framing tries to recast mining an international strait as a legal regime rather than an attack on shipping. How does that sit with UNCLOS transit-passage rights? Would Beijing ever endorse it, given China's own dependence on Gulf oil?

Russian aircraft violate Japanese airspace, then skirt Taiwan, on eve of APEC

What happened
  • On 17 Nov two aircraft believed to be Russian entered Japanese airspace off Okinoshima, Fukuoka Prefecture. Japan scrambled fighters. Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara called it a "serious infringement of Japan's sovereignty" and lodged a strong protest. Japan's last confirmed Russian airspace violation was off Hokkaido in September 2024. Some reports say this route has not been flown since 2013.
  • The aircraft then crossed the Tsushima Strait and passed between Yonaguni and Taiwan into the Pacific. Taiwan's defence ministry tracked two Russian military aircraft off its east coast, which are probably the same pair. Neither government named the aircraft type. South Korean tracking was also reported.
  • Separately, SCMP reports satellite imagery showing nearly 300 PLA Air Force aircraft across 11 bases in western China during an expanded annual exercise.
Why it matters for Australia

Russia is showing reach into the first island chain, along the same sea lanes Australia relies on, just as Putin arrives in China. A route east of Taiwan into the Pacific also shows growing Russian comfort operating near PLA areas of interest. That matters for ADF planning on Russia–China military coordination, which has so far mostly meant joint bomber patrols.

State media framing · Moscow and Beijing

Moscow: no Russian statement was found. Xinhua noted that Moscow had not responded to Japan's protest. Beijing: Xinhua reported Japan's protest flatly, without comment. On the same day it highlighted three US servicemen arrested in Japan over alcohol-related incidents in four days. That steers attention to US forces in Japan rather than the Russian incursion.

Angle to build

Was this a deliberate signal to Takaichi's government, which Lavrov attacked the same day over Japan's non-nuclear principles, timed for maximum publicity before Shenzhen? And how far does Beijing tolerate Russian military activity around Taiwan?

Ukraine drops an arch of the Crimean Bridge as both sides set drone records

What happened
  • Ukraine struck the Crimean (Kerch) Bridge on 16 and 17 Nov. Zelensky says Neptune and Flamingo missiles, with Palianytsia and Ruta drones, were used in the final stage of a weeks-long SBU and General Staff operation, and that one arch collapsed. Photos show a span in the water.
  • Russia closed the road section. Deputy PM Khusnullin says a repair timetable will be set and cargo is moving by other routes. TASS says rail traffic has fully resumed. Peskov says the bridge will be rebuilt "even better than before".
  • Moscow Mayor Sobyanin says about 800 drones aimed at the Moscow region were destroyed overnight. MoD Russia claims a record 1,441 drones intercepted in one night. Ukraine also hit a Gazprom refinery in Bashkortostan.
  • Russia claims it captured Zalivnoye in Zaporizhzhia region and struck substations, ports and rail. A Russian An-12 crashed in Kemerovo, reportedly killing 13 to 17 crew.
  • On diplomacy, Ushakov says Russia–US–Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi will continue. Peskov says Putin may raise a "mission in Konstantinovka" with Trump. Breaking Defense reports Moscow wants Finland to leave NATO as part of a deal.
Why it matters for Australia

Australia is a sanctions partner and supplies military aid to Ukraine, including Bushmasters and Abrams tanks. If Ukraine has a long-range strike capability of its own, Kyiv has more leverage just as Trump, Putin and Xi meet. A Shenzhen understanding on Ukraine that sidelines Europe would also reset the terms of Australia's support.

State media framing · Moscow

TASS reported only a "repeated" drone attack with suspended traffic, and did not address the structural damage. Rybar calls damage images AI-doctored disinformation, yet the Kremlin admits a formal repair plan is needed. Rybar is openly urging strikes on Kyiv's Dnipro bridges for "psychological" effect. More broadly, Russian outlets cast Europe as the spoiler: Ushakov says Europe must decide "war or peace". RT amplifies a report that French troops are operating inside Ukraine. Rybar recasts Kallas's remark about Russian soldiers' data as EU "hacking", and Duma Speaker Volodin threatened her with 20 years in prison. TASS did not mention the final toll of 72 from Russia's Komyshuvakha strike.

Angle to build

Does the bridge strike strengthen Kyiv's hand before Shenzhen, or give Moscow a reason to harden its terms? Compare how Russian outlets handled this attack with the 2022 and 2025 bridge attacks.

PNG tells Xi closing Taiwan's office 'wasn't easy'; Cyclone Xavier hits the Cook Islands

What happened
  • In Shenzhen, PNG Prime Minister Marape told Xi that closing Taiwan's representative office in PNG "wasn't easy". He said he faced "challenges from within" and added: "We hope this assists you in your efforts for reunification." Xi praised PNG for actively developing relations with China.
  • Taiwan shut the office earlier this month and stopped buying PNG LNG, blaming Chinese pressure. The US State Department called the closure "yet another example of Beijing's intimidation campaign".
  • China's top political adviser met Nauru's president. SCMP reports China has run its first large-scale seabed ore mining trial in international waters of the eastern Pacific.
  • Category 5 Cyclone Xavier, with gusts up to 315 km/h, is bearing down on Pukapuka and Nassau in the Northern Cook Islands. No casualties have been reported yet. A relief response may follow.
Why it matters for Australia

PNG is Australia's closest neighbour and is bound to it by the 2025 Pukpuk mutual defence treaty. Marape publicly endorsing Beijing's "reunification" goal goes further than the usual one-China wording. It shows the limits of Canberra's influence over PNG's diplomacy, even with the treaty in place. Cyclone Xavier is a likely test of how quickly Australia and New Zealand can respond to a disaster in the Cook Islands, where China has been building ties.

State media framing · Beijing

Xinhua and People's Daily present the Marape and Nauru meetings as China being a partner to the Pacific, and say nothing about Taiwan's office closing. A Xinhua interview with an Australian business leader, describing APEC Shenzhen as a window to "see the future", is aimed at soft influence in Australia. No state media comment on Cyclone Xavier was found.

Angle to build

Marape's "reunification" line comes about a year after he signed the Pukpuk mutual defence treaty with Australia. Is PNG hedging between security with Australia and diplomatic alignment with China, and can Canberra live with that split?
